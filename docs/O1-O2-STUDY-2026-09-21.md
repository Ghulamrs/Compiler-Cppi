# What /O1 and /O2 are, asked of the vendors

cxx1 spells its levels `-O1` and `-O2` the way cl does, and 21379a8 records an
attempt to give them cl's meaning that came out worse on both axes. Before a
second attempt, the question was put to the two compilers on the Windows box
rather than to memory: **what does each vendor mean by the two levels, what
does each level expand to, and what does the difference cost and buy?**
Microsoft's cl 19.44.35228 (Visual Studio 2022 17.14) and TI's cl6x 8.2.2 (CCS
7.4 at `C:\ti`) answered, on 2026-09-21. The scaffold is
`tools/windows/o1-o2/`; the numbers below are its `results/log.txt`.

## Verdict

**The two vendors do not mean the same thing by `-O1` and `-O2`.**

- **Microsoft: the number is an axis.** `/O1` and `/O2` are the *same*
  optimizer, fully on, pointed at space or at speed. Proven by listing
  identity over the sixteen Compiler++ units: `/O2` is exactly `/O1 /Oi /Ot`,
  and `/O2 /Os` is exactly `/O1 /Oi`. Two switches separate them - `/Os`
  against `/Ot`, and `/Oi` - and nothing else.
- **TI: the number is an amount.** `-O0` to `-O3` are cumulative layers
  (register allocation, then local, then global with software pipelining,
  then file-level). Space against speed is a *separate* switch,
  `--opt_for_space`/`-ms` or its successor `--opt_for_speed`/`-mf`, and TI's
  guide says in a boxed note: *"To reduce code size, do not lower the level
  of optimization."* This is GNU's model, not Microsoft's.

So the user's understanding - `-O1` is size, `-O2` is speed - is exactly
Microsoft's, and cl's own help says so in those words. And the 21379a8
failure is explained: what was built was a *smaller amount* of optimization at
`-O1` (TI's model), measured against Microsoft's expectation that `-O1` is the
*same amount pointed at size*. Less work is not a favour to space.

## 1. Microsoft cl

### 1.1 The definition, in cl's words

`cl -help`, the OPTIMIZATION block, verbatim:

    /O1 maximum optimizations (favor space) /O2 maximum optimizations (favor speed)
    /Ob<n> inline expansion (default n=0)   /Od disable optimizations (default)
    /Og enable global optimization          /Oi[-] enable intrinsic functions
    /Os favor code space                    /Ot favor code speed
    /Ox optimizations (favor speed)
    /GF enable read-only string pooling     /Gy[-] separate functions for linker

"Maximum optimizations" on both lines; only the parenthesis differs.

### 1.2 The expansion, proven rather than quoted

The sixteen Compiler++ units (`AST` .. `VM`, the compiler that is TriLab's
Windows program), each compiled with `-FA` under ten spellings and the listings
compared byte for byte after the file-name comments were dropped:

| spelling                          | listing            | .text of the exe |
|-----------------------------------|--------------------|-----------------:|
| `/Od`                             | 763,166 lines      |          716,052 |
| `/O1`                             | 578,159            |          519,348 |
| `/Og /Os /Oy /Ob2 /GF /Gy`        | **identical to /O1** |        519,348 |
| `/O2`                             | 692,366            |          623,092 |
| `/Og /Oi /Ot /Oy /Ob2 /GF /Gy`    | **identical to /O2** |        623,092 |
| `/O1 /Oi /Ot`                     | **identical to /O2** |        623,092 |
| `/O1 /Oi`                         | 577,378            |          518,756 |
| `/O2 /Os`                         | **identical to /O1 /Oi** |    518,756 |
| `/Ox`                             | 683,301            |          646,196 |
| `/O2 /Ob1`                        | 681,154            |          619,844 |

The documented expansions hold to the byte. `/Ox` is not `/O2`: it lacks
`/GF /Gy`, and without `/Gy` the linker cannot fold, so it is 23 KB larger.
`/O2` is 20.0% more `.text` than `/O1` on this program.

### 1.3 What the difference costs and buys

`compilerpp.exe -run bench.cpp`, a loop-and-call program that runs the
interpreter to its 50-million-step limit, so every build does the same work.
Best of ten:

| build          | ms  | against /O1 |
|----------------|----:|------------:|
| `/Od`          | 1498 |   +284%    |
| `/O1`          |  390 |     -      |
| `/O2`          |  371 |   -4.9%    |
| `/O1 /Oi`      |  415 |   +6.4%    |
| `/O2 /Os`      |  415 |   +6.4% (the same program as /O1 /Oi) |
| `/O2 /Ob1`     |  394 |   +1.0%    |

`/Ot` buys 4.9% on an interpreter loop for 20% of `.text`. `/Oi` alone, at
`/Os`, makes the program 0.1% smaller and 6.4% *slower* here - the intrinsic
expansion of `memcpy` into `movups` runs is not a win when the surrounding code
is tuned for size.

### 1.4 What `/Ot` actually changes

`probe.c` (in the scaffold) holds the patterns where a space and a speed
compiler are expected to part. The whole file, `.text` bytes:

| `/Od` | `/O1` | `/O1 /Oi` | `/O2` | `/O2 /Oi-` | `/Os` alone | `/Ot` alone |
|------:|------:|----------:|------:|-----------:|------------:|------------:|
|   684 |   226 |       246 |   431 |        405 |         600 |         684 |

`/Os` and `/Ot` on their own are nearly `/Od`: they steer an optimizer that
`/Og` has to turn on. Function by function, `/O1` against `/O2`:

- **Identical** under both: `div7`, `mod10` (magic-number multiply), `mul9`
  (`lea`), `mul100` (`imul`), `sel` (`cmov`), `sw` (subtract chain), `f1`..`f3`,
  `len` (`jmp strlen` - no intrinsic for `strlen` on x64 at either level).
  Instruction selection is the same optimizer's.
- **`sum` over `n` ints:** `/O1` is one four-instruction loop. `/O2` is a
  vectorised loop unrolled by two (`movdqu`/`paddd` pairs), then a scalar
  loop unrolled by two for the remainder, then a final element, with `npad`
  before each loop head and a `pdata`/`xdata` chain because the function now
  saves `rbx`. 24 instructions became 60.
- **`sum8` over eight ints:** `/O1` a two-trip vector loop; `/O2` the loop
  peeled flat.
- **`copy16`/`clear16` (64-byte `memcpy`/`memset`):** `/O1` a tail call to the
  library; `/O2` eight `movups` / a `xorps` and four `movups`. This one is
  `/Oi`'s, not `/Ot`'s: `/O1 /Oi` expands `memcpy` too, and `/O2 /Oi-` calls
  it again.
- **`npad`:** 0 at `/O1`, 2 at `/O2` - loop heads are aligned only for speed.
  Function entries are aligned at both.

Over the sixteen units the same three levers account for the whole gap:
loop labels 924 -> 1130, `npad` 2,420 -> 2,961, SSE moves 2,699 -> 6,321,
`cmov` 1,171 -> 1,900; and 11,583 -> 12,053 function bodies, the `/Ot`
inliner accepting more.

**So Microsoft's speed level is three things:** loop unrolling with a
vectorised body and a remainder, inline expansion of fixed-size block moves,
and alignment of loop heads - plus a looser inlining threshold. Everything
else is the same code at both levels.

## 2. TI cl6x

### 2.1 The definition, in cl6x's words

`cl6x --help`, the Optimization Options block:

    -O,--opt_level[=off,0,1,2,3] Optimization level (argument optional, defaults to: 2)
    --opt_for_speed,-mf[=0-5]    Speed vs. size trade-offs  (argument optional, defaults to: 4)
    -ms,--opt_for_space[=0-3]    Optimize for code size (argument optional, defaults to: 0)

The C6000 Optimizing Compiler User's Guide (SPRUI04B, May 2017, section 4.1)
defines the levels as layers: `-O0` register allocation, CFG simplification,
loop rotation; `-O1` adds local copy/constant propagation and local CSE;
`-O2` adds software pipelining, loop optimizations, global CSE, loop
unrolling; `-O3` adds file-level work - dead functions removed, small
functions inlined, arguments propagated. Section 4.5 then:

> To reduce code size, do not lower the level of optimization. Instead, use
> the --opt_for_space option to control the code size/performance tradeoff.
> Higher optimization levels (--opt_level or -O) combined with high
> --opt_for_space levels result in the smallest code size.

and section 4.17 defines `--opt_for_speed=0..5` as a single axis from "code
size with a high risk to performance" (0) to "performance with a high risk to
code size" (5), default 4, with `-ms0..3` mapped onto it as `-mf3..0`.

### 2.2 Measured

`probe.c` under cl6x, `-mv6740 --abi=eabi`, `.text` bytes from `ofd6x`:

| `-Ooff` | `-O0` | `-O1` | `-O2` | `-O3` | `-O2 -ms0` | `-O2 -ms1` | `-O2 -ms2` | `-O2 -ms3` | `-O2 -mf0` | `-O2 -mf5` | `-O0 -ms3` |
|--------:|------:|------:|------:|------:|-----------:|-----------:|-----------:|-----------:|-----------:|-----------:|-----------:|
|     544 |   384 |   416 |   384 |   384 |        416 |        352 |        320 |        320 |        320 |        384 |        352 |

The level barely moves the size of a file with no pipelinable loop; the axis
moves it by a sixth. What `-ms1` and above do is what `/Os` does in spirit:
`div7` and `mod10` stop being inline multiply sequences and become calls to
`__c6xabi_divi` and `__c6xabi_remu`; `memset` stays a call. `-O3` alone
inlines `f1`, `f2` and `memset` into their callers - that is the amount, not
the axis. And `-ms` works at `-O0` too (352), which is the guide's point: the
axis is orthogonal to the level.

## 3. What it means for cxx1

cxx1's driver is cl-shaped and its levels are named cl's way, so **`-O1` and
`-O2` mean Microsoft's axis**: the same passes at both, and a level differs
only where a pass has a genuine size-for-speed choice. 21379a8's rule stands,
now with the vendor's confirmation - a pass that is better on both axes runs
at both levels, and gating one to make the levels differ is TI's model worn
by mistake.

The three places cl draws the line, against what cxx1 has today:

1. **Fixed-size block moves.** cxx1's `copyBlock` (X86_64Linux.cpp) expands
   every struct copy inline, 8 bytes per pair of `mov`s, at every level -
   that is `/Ot`'s answer at both levels. Over the sixteen units at `-O1`
   there are 1,205 such sequences, 3,950 pairs; 1,040 of them are three pairs
   (24-byte objects) and 125 are five or more. A counted loop costs about 21
   bytes whatever the size, against 9 per pair, so a `-O1` that loops from
   three pairs upward would take roughly 10 KB (1.7%) off `.text` and give
   back a loop's speed on every struct copy. This is the one lever that
   exists in the code as it stands, and it is exactly cl's `/Oi` line
   measured above - which also says the gain is small.
2. **Loop-head alignment.** Function entries are `.p2align 4` at every level
   already; loop heads are not aligned at any. `-O2` could align them - cl's
   `npad` - once the walker tells the spelling where a loop head is. Costs
   bytes, buys speed; a real `/Ot` lever, and one the per-run IR cannot see
   by itself.
3. **Unrolling and vectorisation.** Need a loop in the IR. Tier 2.

Until one of these lands, `-O1` and `-O2` stay one program, and the comment
in Optimizer.cpp says so.
